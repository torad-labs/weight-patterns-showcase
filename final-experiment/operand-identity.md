# Which energy term does a subtraction act on?

November 18, 2026. A model can perform friction subtraction correctly while producing an incorrect final energy. This matters for training: the physically required operation is to add gravitational work and subtract dissipated energy. A wrong input carried into the friction step gives a wrong final value even when that step correctly transforms the energy it received.

| Observed fitted checkpoint | Downhill work | Rough-section friction | Available energy |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 387 | 64 − 40 = 24 J (wrong sign) | 24 − 13 = 11 J (correct local operation) | 11 J |
| 388 | 64 + 40 = 104 J (correct sign) | 104 − 13 = 91 J (correct local operation) | 91 J |

In the 30 original answers, the model subtracted in both places on all 15 wrong descents. All 30 correctly subtracted rough-section friction conditional on the energy they supplied to that calculation. The same was true in the reordered example and all four fitted checkpoint captures. These observations support evaluating the two operations separately. The 387→388 intervention history shows that the first behavior changed while the second was preserved in this case. Whether the parameter changes responsible for the two behaviors can be varied independently is open. ## Can a trace distinguish the two roles?

Three previously recorded sources supplied examples of the two operations. Two subtract at both downhill and friction, and the third computes both correctly. These are supervised reference roles. On a query response, every native prediction after the first is classified from its current and preceding computation, without supplying its generated words, position of the physical operation or eventual answer. Donors with the same initial kinetic energy and downhill work as the query are excluded. One matcher compares the direction changes of two successive feed-forward transformations at block 20. The other uses those same distances to align the entire computation prefix since generation began. Other events in the complete donor traces can win as background. Only a downhill call with a positive discrepancy in the 43 following residual merges requests a correction. The comparisons use stored model executions. On the 30 original responses, the local matcher with one reference event per operation flagged **15/15** wrong descents immediately before the model chose the operator. It flagged **0/30** correct friction applications at the equivalent instant and **3/15** valid descents. It caught the reordered error and stayed quiet at that response's friction calculation. It left all three complete correct fitted captures (175, 189 and 388) quiet. It missed the actual downhill subtraction at 387 and instead raised a single alert at 318, immediately before the model added a negligible-friction term. The full-prefix matcher caught 13/15 wrong descents and spared all 15 valid descents at the pre-choice event, but flagged two correct friction decisions on the originals. On the reordered problem it missed the wrong operation and flagged valid friction. The order of operations is shared across these 35 responses; recognizing the phase or equation structure is a competing explanation for apparent operand recognition. ## A full operation spans several transformations

In 387, the nearest prefix had aligned with the reference's downhill equation four events past the reference marker. Calling that background imposed a single-event boundary inside the same physical calculation. A follow-up used each source's independently checked symbolic and numerical applications of each operand as the endpoints of its labeled interval. The query still supplied only its own observed computation. With these intervals, the prefix matcher recognized the failing 387 descent at event 336, before the wrong subtraction at 337, and did not mark any part of its subsequent correct friction interval. It also labeled both operations correctly on 388. But using the same pre-choice downstream discrepancy at later stages in the now-visible interval produced **14 false alerts** during the correct downhill calculation at each of 175, 189 and 388. On the reordered problem, that prefix matcher raised ten alerts inside the correct friction interval. Role recognition and the direction of a corrective request need to be assessed at the same computational stage. ## Does a stage-specific reference cure the false alerts?

A second follow-up held every assigned role and candidate event fixed. For each query event labeled downhill, its closest reference event specified a stage in the downhill calculation. Within each earlier correct or failing witness, the code started from that witness's existing computational landmark and aligned the subsequent turns with the complete reference episode. The conditional downstream score then used the witness transformations at the corresponding stage. Query text and future query events were unnecessary. The donor recordings and their relative distances were observable in full.

With prefix matching, the 14 false downhill alerts on 175, 189 and 388 fell to 11, 11 and 10 respectively. On 387 the revised downstream score stayed quiet before the wrong subtraction at 337. It first alerted at 339, after that choice. At the true precursor (336), the query was paired with event 316 in the correct reference, four events past its pre-choice marker. All 16 eligible witnesses at that matched stage had already chosen their own downhill operator three to seven events earlier. The score changed from +0.02726 with the pre-choice references to −0.75182 with these later references. The broad role was correct while the stage of the computation was misaligned. The rough-friction episode remained quiet on both 387 and 388. On the 15 valid original descents, 12 still received an alert within the downhill episode. On the reordered case the prefix matcher still mislabeled friction and issued four incorrect requests in that interval. The local version caught the reordered error at 258 and spared its entire friction interval, but also marked other parts of its response. A stage-dependent target removed some false requests and lost a real precursor. These scores do not justify installing a weight update. ## Changing later friction sometimes changes the earlier computation

In 18 pairs of natural questions the prompt differed only in the amount of friction loss. Nine pairs reversed the model's downhill choice even though the correct downhill energy was unchanged. Increasing friction repaired five descents and broke four. For example, changing friction from 13 to 24 J switched the model from 64 − 40 to 64 + 40 at the bottom of the same hill. These 18 pairs share 22 questions across nine fixed configurations. In the nine pairs with unchanged sign convention, bottom energy was constant and available energy fell by exactly one joule per additional joule of friction. Yet the change in the 105-merge turn field immediately before the friction operation was larger than at downhill in only four of those nine pairs. Only two pairs isolate a change in drop height; neither has two valid descents. This cohort cannot establish a clean direction for the correct downhill response to added gravitational work. ## How to judge a partial repair

For each tested physical operation, compare the output against what the operation should produce from the inputs the model actually used. Then separately score whether those inputs were already wrong, whether downstream operations were preserved and whether the complete final answer is correct. A wrong final value can be fully explained by an upstream error. A downstream calculation that also violates its own local relation is a candidate second failure. Establish its recurrence on independently varied examples before attributing it to a distinct capability. An unexplained failure of the same downhill calculation remains in scope for the downhill method. Controls where friction itself fails and examples that change the order of operations would test claims beyond these recordings.

    ‖g + h‖² − ‖g‖² = 2〈g,h〉 + ‖h‖²,

the recorded gradient difference has a negative dot product with the ordinary gradient. The unchanged starting state and the single replacement term support interpreting this as opposing contributions. The calculation concerns the recorded gradients with the numerical precision of this implementation. ## The next comparison

1. **Hold the route similar and vary the calculation.** Start with the five wrong and three correct examples above, then test whether the same relation recurs across other natural routes and independent physical configurations. Look for a pre-decision change that differentiates correct and wrong within a route while permitting differences between two correct routes. Compare the incoming residual, gated product, outgoing branch and residual turn already in the record. Previous work found a precursor in several blocks and tested mixtures of correct traces conditional on their incoming computation. Its reliance on individual witnesses warrants leave-one-family-out controls. [Earlier multi-trace analysis](../records/retrospectives/2026-11-17-pre-sign-localization-history-eli.md)
2. **Use the physical response to identify the relevant transition.** In this domain, increasing released energy by ΔD at fixed initial energy must increase bottom kinetic energy by ΔD. Changing only the later climb must leave bottom kinetic energy unchanged. Both positive-release and signed-potential conventions obey these relations. Use natural matched questions as perturbations. Infer the computational phase (forming the bottom energy before friction) from the available trace prefix, allow the method to report ambiguity, and consult independently calculated quantities to evaluate localization. A full-response alignment can use future events. Demonstrating an early warning requires repeating detection without those events. A plus token or a position fixed by text is unsuitable as a training label.
3. **Use several correct traces as conditional targets.** For a matched incoming operation, keep each valid convention’s observed change in the trace, including how that change responds across the paired questions. Ask whether the incorrect response lies outside the correct variation on the physical contrast. Test the registration by matching across correct examples and back, and include examples with the same heading but opposite arithmetic. A good round trip can still align the wrong operations. Freeze candidate directions and registration on discovery cases, check on separate development families, and reserve a fresh set for the final transfer and preservation comparison.
4. **Measure whether an edit can move that signal.** On a frozen history, register the actual reference row and merge and calculate the predicted local change along the whole gradient, first continuously and then using the actual rounded parameter displacement. Compare those predictions to a native measurement on the same history, followed by unconstrained generation from the edited weights. A small internal intervention on a candidate feature with matched sham and unrelated-site controls can test causal relevance before a persistent weight edit. Its effect on the operand interpretation and the downstream arithmetic matters. The target and update still come from computation and the final test remains unassisted.
5. **Choose a change from the failed prediction.** A signal absent before the decision points to representation or registration. A continuous step pointing the wrong way at the target points to the competing objective terms. An effective continuous step that is lost at native precision points to rounding or accumulation. A good local direction with a poor finite response on a fixed history motivates a measured learning-rate adjustment. A successful fixed-history movement followed by an incorrect generated route motivates revisiting decision stability. The learner already applies an empirical multiplier of 0.2698926 to a response-derived step. A smaller multiplier cannot reverse a local first-order direction. [Step rule](../FINAL-EXPERIMENT.md)

## How the literature informs this proposal

- Geiger et al. (2024) test candidate high-level variables using interventions on distributed neural representations. That suggests specifying the counterfactual effect a putative descent variable must have before attempting to train it. Zhang and Nanda show that corruption and measurement choices can change conclusions in activation-patching studies. [Distributed alignment search](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v236/geiger24a.html) · [Activation-patching methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16042)
- Dwibedi et al. (2019) learn temporal correspondence across demonstrations with a cycle-consistency objective. Their work concerns videos. A correct-to-correct round trip is a diagnostic worth adapting to differently worded solutions. [Temporal Cycle-Consistency Learning](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Dwibedi_Temporal_Cycle-Consistency_Learning_CVPR_2019_paper.html)
- Czarnecki et al. (2017) train on function values and target derivatives when those derivatives are available. Here the known derivative is a physical energy relation. Differences between naturally generated internal traces are empirical finite contrasts across discrete prompts. No exact target Jacobian for the hidden model is known. [Sobolev Training for Neural Networks](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/758a06618c69880a6cee5314ee42d52f-Abstract.html)
- Ren et al. (2018) use gradient information from clean validation examples to reweight training examples. This motivates measuring whether a proposed training-example gradient improves the operation on independent development examples. Any examples used to choose gradients or step sizes become development data. [Learning to Reweight Examples for Robust Deep Learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/ren18a.html)

## Which distinction survives before generation feedback?

At the known descent readout on the fitted 91 J question, the early model entered the historical correct range when supplied with the repaired history. Both physically repaired models stayed outside on the original wrong history. At the earlier 73 decoded predictions all five states still received identical inputs. Comparing each edit’s change in turn across all 105 merges, the two successful edits had cosine 0.7364, versus 0.2991 and 0.2006 for the pairings with the failed edit. The repair pair ranked first separately at each of the 73 events. The same ordering held with either common starting model subtracted. At the later descent stage, the simpler mean local squared-distance change even had opposite signs in the two successful edits. Correct friction could show substantial inherited motion.

The early direction is a candidate for ranking future steps. The 73 observations share one prompt, and both repairs share donor ancestry. To qualify it for the learner, freeze it and predict unseen outcomes with computation-based registration and comparable unsuccessful displacements. Score descent and friction separately on the resulting fresh generations. ## What happened when the shared direction entered the update

We froze a direction from the two successful fitted interventions, with a local floor at each of the 7,299 event–merge pairs on which they agreed. Eleven updates from checkpoint 390 differentiated the resulting shortfalls together with the ordinary correct-reference discrepancies. At 390 the model already added 40 J on the fitted descent, leaving 91 J after friction. Only 33 of 7,402 registered contributions were below the floor. The loop preserved the fitted answer, decreased its ordinary loss by 0.6934% and increased its auxiliary loss by 2.4612%. The rounded-step forecast correctly predicted the sign of the auxiliary change on nine of eleven updates; six installed steps were predicted to move it uphill. On four steps the continuous prefix directional derivative was already positive. For those four, shortening the joint step alone offers no predicted prefix improvement in the local model. [Training and component forecasts](../records/development/2026-11-18-prefix-direction-training/README.md).

At baseline, 16.1–21.8% of registered contributions fell below the same directional floor on eight new prompts, compared with 0.45% on the fit. The queries hit different source locations and visited some more often. This contrast demonstrates that the fitting example offers very little remaining directional error while held-out examples carry substantial discrepancy. It does not locate the cause of a wrong downhill operation on those examples. Both blind readers found zero newly correct local descents. All sixteen answers subtracted the stated friction amount from their chosen input, with disagreements over contradictory equations, order and reuse. The model also gave a valid alternative-datum balance on one baseline prompt. The [primary and independent physics readings](../studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/prefix-direction-generalization/paired-independent-audit.json) keep these distinctions visible.

When both checkpoints were compared on the same source cells, held-out prefix loss increased in six of eight pairs. On the 59 J versus 60 J configuration, it increased 17.15% in route order and decreased 2.87% with the endpoint first. Neither ordering acquired the missing operation. On the three prompts where both models produced identical sequences, losses increased 0.38–0.93%; each such comparison also held generated inputs fixed.

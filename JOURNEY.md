## Chapter 1: Orienting in Weight & Activation Space (Nov 5–6)

The project began in [`studies/01-early-exploration/`](studies/01-early-exploration/) by testing how far direct inspection of static weights and single-pass activations could go on Gemma 4 E2B (`35` layers, residual width `1536`, shared KV caches from layers `13/14` into `15–34`, GeGLU MLPs up to width `12288`, and per-layer-input gates):

- In [`decode-observatory`](studies/01-early-exploration/decode-observatory/run/ANALYSIS.md), we captured 30,000 generated tokens across 187 multi-turn steps (decode latency slope $2.725\text{ ms}$ per $1{,}000$ context tokens, $R^2 = 0.999525$), and built [`apps/decode-machine/`](apps/decode-machine/) to inspect cross-layer signals visually instead of relying on summary scalars.
- In [`llamacpp-lookup-comparison`](studies/01-early-exploration/llamacpp-lookup-comparison/run/RESULTS.md), `llama.cpp` answered `20/20` table-lookup prompts concisely and accurately where an earlier quantized QGRE recording returned only `5/20` concise correct answers (`8` numeric substitutions, `4` wrong listed values, and `3` truncated continuations), separating a conversion/runtime artifact from an intrinsic model failure.
- In [`WEIGHT-SPACE-ORIENTATION.md`](studies/01-early-exploration/WEIGHT-SPACE-ORIENTATION.md) (`mechanism-study`), inspecting the failing lookup `Lom = blue -> 2` showed that five of eight query heads in layer 29 already ranked the prompt's `blue` token first, while the largest opposing final-MLP weight-activation products (channel `4302` at $-5.008$ and channel `10884` at $+5.379$) fired even more strongly on *correct* answers (`3.5` on `red`, `-2.75` on valid `blue`).

Consolidated on November 6 in [`hypothesis/RESIDUAL-TRACE-THEORY.md`](hypothesis/RESIDUAL-TRACE-THEORY.md), these negative checks ruled out searching for isolated "bad weights" or static activation templates. Capabilities run horizontally through connected, context-dependent transformations across layers; recognizing recurring residue in live execution traces became the main research thread.

## Chapter 2: Native BF16 Instrumentation & Physics Trace Baselines (Nov 6–7)

To study multi-step reasoning where intermediate steps are explicit and auditable without 4-bit quantization noise (`Q4_K`), we set up a conservation-of-energy physics suite and built custom C++ instrumentation inside our pinned `llama.cpp` engine ([`code/signal-trace/recorder/`](code/signal-trace/recorder/), [`studies/02-physics-trace-observation/`](studies/02-physics-trace-observation/)):

- On the 24-question physics baseline ([`FINDINGS.md`](publications/findings/FINDINGS.md)), moving from mixed `Q4_K` to native `BF16` (`318` BF16 and `283` FP32 tensors = `601` GGUF tensors total, of which `538`—`278` BF16 and `260` FP32—lie on the trace-connected forward path) raised explicit verdicts from `8/24` to `13/24` and supplied-arithmetic accuracy from `1/6` to `4/6`, while complete multi-step physical chains rose only from `0/18` to `2/18` (`0/18` wholly defect-free explanations). The reasoning failures persisted cleanly in full precision.
- We built `observe` and the reduced-retention [`observe_stream.cpp`](code/signal-trace/recorder/observe_stream.cpp) ([`STREAM-RECORDER.md`](code/signal-trace/recorder/STREAM-RECORDER.md)) to record all three residual merges per block (`attn_out`, `pe_in` / FFN merge, and `pe_out` / per-layer-input merge, giving **105 merges per token**) via scheduler callbacks with zero per-op `cudaDeviceSynchronize`. `observe` reproduced ordinary inference bit-for-bit across **13,462** Q4 decisions and **12,023** BF16 decisions (matching every chosen token and full vocabulary logit hash), and `observe_stream` matched bit-for-bit across its **480** residual, **480** projection, and **265** trace-excerpt qualification decisions on GPU0.
- Across 12 paired physics comparisons, relative trajectory separation contracted across FF14 and expanded across FF15 on **846/846** Q4 decisions and **1,707/1,708** BF16 decisions ([`physics-trace-bf16/STUDY.md`](studies/02-physics-trace-observation/physics-trace-bf16/STUDY.md)). However, substituting a single prediction's layer-16 attention contribution ([`physics-intervention/STUDY.md`](studies/02-physics-trace-observation/physics-intervention/STUDY.md), `1,860` donor/recipient decisions) altered current-step scores while changing **0 emitted tokens**.

Because layers `15–34` reuse KV caches written by earlier blocks (`0–14`), a one-step transplant at layer 16 leaves nothing in the KV cache for subsequent decode steps to read. Sustaining a multi-step calculation change required editing the stored BF16 weights so the intervention re-applies across generated positions.

## Chapter 3: Single-Pair Rank-1 Weight Edits & Background Conditioning (Nov 7 · Paper 1)

Round 1 ([`studies/03-round-1-single-pair-edits/`](studies/03-round-1-single-pair-edits/), reported in Paper 1: [`reports/fixing-a-reasoning-mistake-by-editing-the-weights.pdf`](reports/fixing-a-reasoning-mistake-by-editing-the-weights.pdf)) tested a controlled sequence (`A -> B -> C -> D1`) editing only `blk.16.attn_output.weight` ($1536 \times 2048 = 3{,}145{,}728$ BF16 entries) from one paired prompt contrast (`3 kg` block climbing `1 m` [correct: `60 J / 30 J / YES`] vs. `3 m` [wrong: `60 J / 120 J / NO`, double-counting descent height]):

1. **Candidate `A`** ([`A-weight-edit/STUDY.md`](studies/03-round-1-single-pair-edits/A-weight-edit/STUDY.md)): A rank-1 update $\Delta W = (y_{\text{succ}} - y_{\text{fail}})x_{\text{fail}}^\top / \|x_{\text{fail}}\|^2$ rounded to native BF16 ($200{,}965$ entries changed, $0.02966\%$ matrix norm, `0` bytes changed outside `blk.16.attn_output`) repaired the `3 m` block to **`60 / 90 / NO`** under free generation. It computed the recipient's own $3\text{ m} \times 30\text{ N} = 90\text{ J}$ instead of copying the donor's $30\text{ J}$, also repaired a sled prompt (`0/40/NO -> 80/40/YES`), and kept **7/7** prior correct chains (`7/28 -> 9/28` complete chains). However, it broke a `4 kg, 2 m` block (`60/80/NO -> 60/40/YES`).
2. **Candidate `B`** ([`B-selective-footprint`](studies/03-round-1-single-pair-edits/B-selective-footprint/run/REPORT.md)): Suppressing action on 9 protected inputs while anchoring only prediction 235 shrank downstream action at the token-divergence step 269 ($0.7212\% \to 0.06969\%$) and **lost the repair** (`60/120/NO` returned), showing that single-position anchoring does not sustain a multi-step calculation.
3. **Candidate `C`** ([`C-connected-preservation`](studies/03-round-1-single-pair-edits/C-connected-preservation/run/REPORT.md)): Preserving `A`'s action across **304 connected positive inputs** while suppressing the 9 protected inputs kept both repairs (`90 J` and `80 J`), restored the protected `4 kg` block (`60/80/NO`), and preserved all **7/7** original correct chains.
4. **Candidate `D` (`D1`)** ([`D-background-sensitivity`](studies/03-round-1-single-pair-edits/D-background-sensitivity/run/REPORT.md)): Auditing `A` and `C` on unrelated text/math prompts showed the calibration mean vector drove **93.16%** (`A`) and **91.91%** (`C`) of squared update action. Conditioning the rank-1 read with an inverse-second-moment background covariance (`D1`, $331{,}670$ BF16 values changed, $0.04522\%$ matrix norm) kept every repair and preservation property of `C` while reducing RMS background action by **85.50%** on generic requests and **69.65%** on language controls.

Even with `D1`'s selectivity, performance on four physics prompts introduced with `C` and excluded from `D1`'s fit stayed **1/4 -> 1/4** (and an early ad-hoc multi-case candidate `E` was audited and withdrawn in [`2026-11-07-e-withdrawal.md`](journal/2026-11-07-e-withdrawal.md)). A single prompt pair entangles problem-specific numbers with the capability bug; isolating the capability required overlapping many varied executions.

## Chapter 4: Multi-Trace Overlaps & First Generalizing Weight Repairs (Nov 7–10 · Papers 2 & 3)

Moving from one pair to many traces produced numerical transfer within the energy-transfer family and forced stricter experimental controls.

### 4.1 Contextual Multi-Case Edits (`FK` and `old A2`, Paper 2)
Before the Round-2 reset, two multi-matrix edits were fitted across 45 construction questions and tested on **40 excluded questions** with unseen initial speeds ($5, 7\text{ m/s}$ vs. $4, 6, 8\text{ m/s}$ in construction) and unseen final climb heights ($2, 4\text{ m}$ vs. $1, 3\text{ m}$), documented in Paper 2 ([`reports/how-the-trace-derived-edit-works.pdf`](reports/how-the-trace-derived-edit-works.pdf)) and reconstructed from per-question records in [`records/retrospectives/2026-11-09-round-two-repairs/STUDY.md`](records/retrospectives/2026-11-09-round-two-repairs/STUDY.md):
- **`FK` (3 matrices: `blk.11.ffn_down` + `blk.14.attn_k/v`):** Repaired **15/20** wrong or missing required-climb fields on the 40 excluded questions (writing explicit $mgH$ products with the new masses and heights), though collateral descent-sign damage from `F` left complete answers at **2/31** repaired and **8/9** prior correct damaged.
- **`old A2` (46 `attn_output` + `ffn_down` matrices across 23 layers):** Repaired **25/36** broader capability errors (**14/18** targeted climb errors) on the 67-question development set, and repaired **9/31** complete wrong answers plus **15/20** climb fields on the 40 excluded questions (raising total complete triples from **9/40 -> 12/40**, while damaging **6/9** prior correct complete answers). Ablating only the 27 natural-success zero-change rows collapsed development repairs from **25/36 -> 0/36** and damaged **31/31** correct answers, giving concrete evidence that preservation rows shaped a useful correction direction in this fit.

Although `FK` and `old A2` showed numerical transfer within that problem family, their targets came from hand-paired contextual mates and arbitrary thresholds (`1%` gap share, `7.2%` matrix cap, audited in [`2026-11-09-round-two-repairs/STUDY.md`](records/retrospectives/2026-11-09-round-two-repairs/STUDY.md)) instead of an independent multi-trace overlap. Following the November 7 clarifications ([`2026-11-07-clarifications.md`](journal/2026-11-07-clarifications.md), [`2026-11-07-multitrace-purpose.md`](journal/2026-11-07-multitrace-purpose.md), [`2026-11-07-round-two-restart.md`](journal/2026-11-07-round-two-restart.md)), I withdrew that first attempt and put [`round-2-method.json`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/round-2-method.json) and [`verify_round2.py`](code/signal-trace/pipeline/research_guard/verify_round2.py) in place so edit locations had to emerge from 105-merge computational overlap without hand-picked thresholds.

### 4.2 Pure 60-Trace DTW Computational Overlap (`a2-path`, Paper 3)
Under the Round-2 rules, we captured 30 completely correct traces and 30 wrong traces sharing the same first error (subtracting downhill gravitational work $KE_0 - mgd$) on GPU0 (`30,546` total generated token steps) and aligned every trace across all 105 merges by DTW on unit-normalized turn vectors alone ([`a2-path/declaration.json`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/a2-path/declaration.json), [`a2-path/result.json`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/a2-path/result.json), Paper 3: [`reports/how-the-path-edit-works.pdf`](reports/how-the-path-edit-works.pdf)):
- Without any token or position hint, the 60 traces separated in one contiguous computational stretch (aligned places `142–313`) at **5.8$\times$** sampling variation with split-half cosine agreement **0.825**.
- The planned 70-matrix sequential build was halted after the first **18 matrices** (layers `0–8`, before any answer was generated) when layer 9 left the solver's regime. Answering freely, `a2-path` flipped **29/30** fitted wrong answers from subtracting to adding gravitational work (**17/30** complete; **18/30** originally correct kept complete, `12/30` broken; overall `55/60` adding descent vs. `30/60` before). On Round 1's 28-question suite outside the fit, it completed **7/21** previously wrong chains (**11/21** adding descent) while breaking **4/7** prior correct chains (**3/7** kept complete).

## Chapter 5: Falsifications That Changed the Method (Nov 10–15)

`a2-path` fixed the targeted sign in `29/30` cases but introduced new arithmetic regressions (such as misreporting post-climb energy as available energy or halving/doubling initial kinetic energy) and reworded `49/60` openings. Between November 10 and November 15, we tested and ruled out four candidate fixes (cataloged in [`studies/04-round-2-overlap/RUN-CATALOG.md`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/RUN-CATALOG.md)):

1. **Post-hoc protection rungs (`b2-path`, `c2-path`, `d2-path`) and FP32 detours** ([`2026-11-10-controlled-training.md`](journal/2026-11-10-controlled-training.md), [`2026-11-11-what-made-the-edits-work`](records/retrospectives/2026-11-11-what-made-the-edits-work/README.md)): Follow-on rungs all scored below `a2-path`'s `17/30` wrong + `18/30` right complete chains (`b2-path` `4/30` + `9/30`, `c2-path` `12/30` + `16/30`, `d2-path` `7/30` + `7/30`), and post-hoc rescaling/refitting attempts degraded performance as well. Promoting editable matrices to FP32 hid native quantization behavior and was rejected; updates had to work inside native BF16 storage.
2. **Per-layer variance tubes (`controlled-bf16-1..4`)** ([`2026-11-12-correction-not-protection.md`](journal/2026-11-12-correction-not-protection.md)): Enforcing hard per-matrix row/mean variance bounds (`tube.py`) treated intermediate state spread as fixed. Either BF16 rounding erased the constrained step, or downstream blocks could not compensate for upstream adjustments. We replaced separate protection penalties with one correction objective: zero direct gradient where computation agrees within natural witness variation, positive gradient only where it diverges.
3. **Teacher-forced single sweeps and lexical/token judges** ([`2026-11-14-iterative-gap-closure.md`](journal/2026-11-14-iterative-gap-closure.md)): Route audits ([`sign-route-a2-path`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/sign-route-a2-path/result.json)) showed part of `a2-path`'s `+` rode on shifted opening wording, and teacher-forced replays diverged from free generation. All word, token-logit, and parsed-answer progress instruments were removed from the trainer so the model had to regenerate freely after each update and be judged strictly on its 105-merge trace.
4. **Post-merge state-value targets (`success-sets-loop-from-mates`)** ([`2026-11-15-computation-not-state-values.md`](journal/2026-11-15-computation-not-state-values.md)): Because pulling post-merge residual vectors $h^{\text{out}}$ toward convex hulls of successful answers can carry donor problem content into the recipient, [`success-sets-loop-from-mates`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/success-sets-loop-from-mates/declaration.json) was stopped on that concern at write 42 of 70 (the partial model was not measured). Next we specified the relational branch turn $T_{j,m} = \hat{y}_{j,m} - \hat{x}_{j,m}$ calibrated against $K = 14$ non-self witness traces ([`vector-chance-original`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/vector-chance-original/README.md)), later removing the tolerance band for group contrast on November 18.

## Chapter 6: Iterative Computational Backprop, Causal Transplants & Operation Signals (Nov 16–22 · Paper 4)

### 6.1 Direct Computational Backpropagation (`0 -> 390`, Paper 4)
Initially, step 5 ran an inner conjugate-gradient least-squares (`CGLS`) linear inverse per step. On November 16 ([`2026-11-16-direct-computational-update.md`](journal/2026-11-16-direct-computational-update.md), [`reports/Iterative_Learning_from_Computational_Traces.docx`](reports/Iterative_Learning_from_Computational_Traces.docx)), we replaced inner linear inversion with iterative response-scaled weight-gradient descent over the **70** declared projection matrices (`0 -> 317`), and on November 17 ([`FINAL-EXPERIMENT.md`](FINAL-EXPERIMENT.md)) widened backpropagation to all **538 eligible native parameter tensors** (`317 -> 390`):
- Compute VJP gradient $S = -\nabla_W L(W)$ and JVP response $q = J_r S$ through the 35-block PyTorch graph ([`torch_forward.py`](records/development/2026-11-12-path-forward/torch_forward.py), [`full_graph.py`](code/signal-trace/pipeline/weight_edit/full_graph.py)), apply $\Delta W = \eta\alpha S$ with $\alpha = -\langle r, q\rangle / \langle q, q\rangle$ and $\eta = 0.26989$, carry FP32 rounding remainders ([`full_state.py`](code/signal-trace/pipeline/weight_edit/full_state.py)), regenerate freely in `llama.cpp`, and realign via DTW.
- While diagnosing finite-forecast discrepancies against native `llama.cpp`, factorial Q/K/V substitutions traced a sign flip to FP16 KV-cache quantization in early attention producers. Anchoring original native keys across all 15 KV producers ([`native_key_anchor.py`](code/signal-trace/pipeline/weight_edit/native_key_anchor.py), [`2026-11-18-installed-native-key-anchor`](records/development/2026-11-18-installed-native-key-anchor/README.md)) corrected the sign of the finite forecast and cut projection error by **90.66%** on the primary displacement (`-9.94%` on a second displacement, while the unanchored rounded JVP kept the wrong sign).
- On `discovery-05-channel-h1` ([`reports/descent-trace-repair/paper.pdf`](reports/descent-trace-repair/paper.pdf), [`training-and-stopping.md`](final-experiment/training-and-stopping.md)), fresh computational loss fell **77.95%** by update `317` (`0.00029234 -> 0.00006447`, with `91 J` already present by checkpoint `174/175`) and **78.09%** by checkpoint `390` (`0.00006406`), repairing `64 - 40 - 13 = 11 J (NO)` to **`64 + 40 - 13 = 91 J (YES)`** while preserving rough-patch friction (`-13 J`) and the `44 J` reference chain (`expanded-004-h1`). Discrete route switches caused relapses at updates `321–323`, `345`, `356`, `371`, and `387`, each recovered on subsequent updates (`324`, `346`, `357`, `372`, `388`). Checkpoints `175`, `189`, and `348` also repaired the climb requirement on the unfitted `115 J` problem (`60 -> 30 J`). On a checkpoint-blind 8-formulation unseen energy panel ([`paired-outcomes.json`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/frozen-energy-generalization/paired-outcomes.json)), complete valid chains rose **`1/8` (stock) -> `2/8` (`175/189`) -> `3/8` (`390`)** (two wrong chains and one length-capped chain became correct; the one baseline success regressed), while correct downhill signs moved **`4/8 -> 3/8`** (one repaired, two regressed).

### 6.2 Causal Weight-Family Transplants & Operation-Specific Trace Signals
To understand how the learner repaired `91 J` and why single-reference training did not yet fix every unseen descent, we ran targeted causal transplants and operation-level overlap studies:
- **Redundant causal sufficiency across weight families** ([`2026-11-17-descent-sign-swap/README.md`](records/development/2026-11-17-descent-sign-swap/README.md), [`vector-chance-descent-sign-swap/result.json`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/vector-chance-descent-sign-swap/result.json)): Transplanting *only* the 35 `attn_output` matrices ($785{,}007$ BF16 diffs) or *only* the 35 `ffn_down` matrices ($1{,}368{,}221$ BF16 diffs) from a repaired descendant (`174`) onto the failing `early-27` model (`11 J`) each restored `64 + 40 = 104 J` and `91 J / YES` on its own, recovering **99.84%** and **99.21%** of the full trace-loss reduction. Across all 73 shared-prefix predictions before token divergence, the two transplants (which share a training lineage) shifted the 105-merge turn field along a common direction (cosine **0.7364**) that a failed edit missed (**0.2991 / 0.2006**, [`same-input-operation-comparison`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/same-input-operation-comparison/README.md)); at the later block-20 site after histories diverged, they agreed at cosine **0.9991**.
- **105-merge conditional climb signal** ([`joint-climb-conditional-overlap`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/joint-climb-conditional-overlap/README.md)): Conditioning across 16 downhill-subtracting traces isolated a retrospective 712-cell 105-merge climb contrast that separated the withheld `115 J` extra-height error on **643/673** cells and dropped **86.9%** ($0.06863 \to 0.00899$ across `670` shared cells) when checkpoint `175` repaired its climb ($60 \to 30\text{ J}$).
- **Pre-choice descent vs. friction localization** ([`operand-identity.md`](final-experiment/operand-identity.md), [`landmark-route-comparison.md`](final-experiment/landmark-route-comparison.md), [`unbanded-control-readback`](records/development/2026-11-18-unbanded-control-readback/README.md), [`pure-descent-native-read`](records/retrospectives/2026-11-18-pure-descent-native-read/README.md)): Every stock answer that subtracted gravitational work still subtracted friction correctly. Local two-turn matching on 30 stock responses flagged **15/15** wrong descents, **3/15** valid descents, and **0/30** valid friction steps; refined landmark-consensus donor registration ([`registration-results.json`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/descent-landmark-route-comparison/registration-results.json)) flagged **13/13** wrong and **0/15** valid descents at the pre-choice event (while still alerting elsewhere on full-response scans) and caught the reordered error (`discovery-02-reordered-h3`). On the two causal transplants, unbanded group downhill loss dropped **99.981% / 99.990%** while friction error rose **43.79% / 77.22%**. A 1-step isolated FFN20 pullback on `early-27` moved the native `+` vs. `-` logit margin on fixed history from **$-2.5242 \to -0.0465$**, though `-` stayed greedy and free generation routed back to `-8.85`. Adding an unweighted auxiliary prefix term onto `390 -> 401` yielded `0/8` new unseen repairs (`390` also `0/8` on that `±1 J` panel, Paper 4 §12).

### 6.3 Diagnosing the Unanchored Joint Loop & Next Experiment (Nov 18–22)
On November 18–19, `joint-stock-prechoice-training` tried to repair both stock failures simultaneously (`91 J` descent and `95 J` climb, keeping `44 J` intact) from unedited stock weights ([`fresh-fit-readback.json`](studies/04-round-2-overlap/runs/joint-stock-prechoice-training/fresh-fit-readback.json), [`2026-11-21-consolidation-takeover.md`](journal/2026-11-21-consolidation-takeover.md)):
- Update 1 fixed the `95 J` required climb ($120 \to \mathbf{40\text{ J}}$) and cut pre-choice squared error by **28.03%**, while breaking that question's available energy (`95 -> 15 J`) and the `91 J` climb (`20 -> 60 J`); logged group loss then fell ($0.0272 \to 0.0174$ by revision 3) while wrong descents spread from **1/3 -> 3/3** answers (`2` newly broken).
- Diagnosis from the recheck ([`2026-11-22-joint-loss-recheck/README.md`](records/development/2026-11-22-joint-loss-recheck/README.md)):
  1. **Missing whole-trace anchor in the gradient:** `group_contrast_driver.py` differentiated only the sparse labelled-operation cells while logging the whole-trace reference loss passively; that whole-trace loss rose **13$\times$** ($0.000292 \to 0.00387$) and preserved-operation error rose **51$\times$** ($0.00034 \to 0.01753$), motivating the hypothesis that the whole-trace reference is needed in the gradient to keep shared weights on-manifold.
  2. **Matcher dropout:** Whenever the stage matcher missed a degraded operation, `group_contrast_objective.py` contributed `0` to the logged loss. Under a last-measured-value charge on the MLflow step records, Update 1 was **-28.6%**, whereas subsequent updates pushed carried loss to **+22.6%** above stock (**+42.0%** on the `3,845` common cells).

After the November 21–22 archive verification restored all digest-verified captures and blind panel records, shipped all 139 files of the Paper 4 release list, and recorded 63 unarchived files that no store holds by SHA-256 in [`recorded-losses.json`](records/maintenance-runs/2026-11-19-evening-studies-deletion/recorded-losses.json) (`76` standalone guard tests in `make test-guard`), the next experiment is concrete: return the differentiated whole-trace reference anchor to the gradient alongside calibrated operation-specific terms, score and stop on carried loss where unaligned stages keep their last measured penalty, and run the matched token-loss twin control ([`journal/2026-11-22-token-loss-twin.md`](journal/2026-11-22-token-loss-twin.md)).

## Retrospective: Conducting Empirical Research with Coding Agents

I ran this eighteen-day study solo—owning the hypotheses, mathematical formulations, experimental design, and every scientific pivot myself—while heavily directing **frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI as a collaborative coding and verification team** (parallel implementation peers for C++ `llama.cpp` hooks, PyTorch VJP/JVP graphs, verification harnesses, and plotting scripts, paired with independent source-bound `Eli` reviews against Papers 1–4). Working that closely with frontier agents on an open-ended empirical question surfaced three recurring failure modes that shaped how this repository is built:

- **Proxy regression:** Even under explicit instructions to compare internal computation, agents repeatedly drifted back toward surface tokens, wording alignment, teacher-forced replay, and unpenalized matcher-dropout metrics. In two concrete checks, wording moved an early trace descriptor **40$\times$** more than right-vs-wrong divergence (`0.196` vs. `0.0043`), and matcher dropout disguised a **+22.6%** carried-loss increase as a **-25.7%** logged drop.
- **Shortcuts and software over-engineering:** When native constraints got hard, agents reached for escape hatches (upcasting BF16 weights to FP32 instead of fixing remainder installation, freezing 69 of 70 matrices while computing traces across 35 layers, or spending hours building inner linear solvers and per-step 9.3 GB checkpoint pipelines before testing a single weight update).
- **Sycophancy and defensive bureaucracy:** Neutral clarifying questions occasionally caused an agent to abandon a valid running experiment as "broken," reviewer agents folded under builder pushback before measurement confirmed the reviewer was right, and long-horizon sessions sometimes stalled overnight on unasked edge-case gates instead of advancing the test.

Three operational habits came out of those failures and are now enforced in code here:
1. **Remove proxy instruments structurally** (`forbidden_bytes.py`, `verify_round2.py`) instead of layering verbal rules on top of tools that still measure tokens or surface verdicts.
2. **Test the measure before building the pipeline**, including carried penalties for unaligned steps so lost alignment cannot read as lower loss.
3. **Ask agents to reason about falsifiers and audit raw artifacts independently**, instead of stacking rigid checklists that invite compliance theater over honest measurement.

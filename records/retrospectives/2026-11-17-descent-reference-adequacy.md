# What the selected reference establishes about descent

The selected original `expanded-004-h1` supplies the correct numerical descent operation, but it does not establish an operand-independent or wording-independent rule in the trained model. The unchanged one-pair experiment remains a valid test of learning toward this fixed computational reference. The current development observation shows incomplete transfer of one specific calculation: discovery-10 still subtracts the descent energy, even after its climb calculation and final verdict improve. It does not show that the fixed reference is invalid, or that learning failed on discovery-05.

This assessment reads the exact 30 development originals named by `functional-layout-original/definition.json`, their existing response records, the three-checkpoint development profile, and historical diagnostic summaries. It performs no model pass, trace fit, reference replacement or new control selection. No sealed records or raw no-fit responses are opened. The companion JSON binds every original response used. Historical auxiliary-prefix summaries are identified separately below; they are not added to the 30-member analysis or used to calibrate a target.

The common physical relation is `available = m*v²/2 + m*g*drop − dissipation`, with `required = m*g*rise` and g=10. All three questions explicitly describe descent followed by a rough horizontal section and a climb whose height is measured above the bottom of that climb. There is no physical ambiguity requiring subtraction of the descent or addition of the earlier drop to the final climb height. Their generated explanations introduce that confusion themselves.

The selected reference explicitly adds 16+40, then subtracts 12 and compares 44 with 20. Its phrase “Potential Energy gained” during descent is misleading: the potential energy is converted to kinetic energy. That phrase does not make its numerical addition incorrect. Conversely, nominally successful originals can mislabel intermediate states: discovery-00-h3 first says bottom kinetic energy stays 16 J, then adds 20 J as “Total Mechanical Energy”; discovery-15-h1 similarly preserves 72 J at an intermediate bottom-state label before adding 80 J. The reference is numerically suitable for this selected operation, not certified flawless in every word or internal computation. No external reading of these explanations identifies the exact internal representation of gravity or the scope of the learned capability.

## Existing counterexamples distinguish operands from context

The 30 recorded originals contain 15 explicit descent subtractions among the historically wrong members and 15 net additions among the historically correct members. The former include the incomplete, token-capped expanded-001-h3: its observed subtraction is evidence, not a completed answer. This is a selected development cohort, not an estimated error rate.

Several existing pairs already contradict a deterministic account based only on the descent operands:

- Discovery-10-h1 subtracts 96−30, whereas discovery-10-h3 adds 96+30. Mass, initial speed, drop and friction are identical; only the later climb changes from 1 m to 3 m. The earlier descent calculation should be unchanged. This is the most direct counterexample relevant to the current nontransfer.
- Discovery-05-h1 subtracts 64−40, whereas expanded-023-h1 adds 64+40. The only numerical question difference is friction, 13 J versus 24 J. Expanded-020-h1, with friction 12 J, subtracts the same operands.
- Discovery-17-h1 and expanded-068/070-h1 subtract 128−80; expanded-071-h1 adds 128+80. Their differing friction values should not change the preceding descent sign.
- Discovery-11-h3 adds 96+60; expanded-046-h3 subtracts the same operands, with friction 15 J versus 20 J.

The initial-energy regime is associated with the selected examples but cannot determine the sign. The selected reference has 16<40, while discovery-05 and discovery-10 have initial energy above the descent gain. Yet expanded-001-h3 subtracts 16−20 and expanded-087-h3 subtracts 90−100, while discovery-10-h3 correctly adds despite 96>30. The latter and expanded-071-h1 were already explicit counterexamples in the historical same-line diagnostic. An explanation that the model simply avoids a negative bottom energy is therefore insufficient.

The generated labels are also insufficient. Both addition and subtraction occur after misleading “Bottom of descent”/“Top of descent” labels; both occur after “Potential Energy gained.” Correct discovery-06-h1 adds 24+30 despite reversed state labels. These are possible context cues, not established causes. The counterexample pairs have different full prompts and potentially different generated prefixes. They are not identical-input or fixed-history interventions.

## What is known at the current one-pair checkpoints

The frozen development profile at 175 and 189, together with the separate 348 diagnostic, retains correct reference arithmetic (44/20/YES) and fitted discovery-05 arithmetic (91/20/YES). On discovery-10, all three edited checkpoints give 55 J available and 30 J required, with YES. The original gave 55 J and 60 J, with NO. Correcting the climb height and verdict therefore did not repair the descent: 55 comes from 96−30−11; the physically correct available energy is 115 J. A YES-only assessment would hide the remaining failure.

The later native descent-family intervention is complementary evidence on the fitted case. Starting from a retained early model that subtracts 64−40, either the later attention-output family or the later FFN-down family independently restores addition and the complete 91/20/YES calculation. These are whole-family sufficiency results at those exact backgrounds; they do not identify a unique responsible weight, repairing training update, or transfer mechanism. The early fresh control has a different history and scalar loss from its historical acquisition but reproduces its wrong arithmetic; the reported transplant effects compare the four fresh same-device conditions.

## Existing controlled-history evidence and its limits

`sign-route-whole-gap-mates/result.json` records an earlier, distinct model edited by the now-withdrawn multi-answer method. On the same original sign histories, those edited weights move all 15 wrong cases toward addition and flip 12; all 15 correct cases retain addition. Conversely, original weights on that edited model's new histories favor addition in 10 of the 15 wrong cases. Discovery-05 and discovery-10 are among cases supporting both routes. Thus the repository already contains direct evidence that a weight change can alter the sign at fixed history, and that changing the supplied history can alter it at fixed original weights. It does not establish either effect for today's one-pair checkpoint. The second sign between already-substituted numerals was positive under both weights on all 30 rows after the symbolic sign had been consumed; inspecting only that later sign would not distinguish the failure.

The historical `same-prefix-135-original` and `same-prefix-contributions-original` aggregate reports describe five variants with m=3, v=8, drop=1, rise=1 and losses 11,12,16,20,24. The first two choose subtraction and the last three addition after the same generated prefix. Their question number differs, so the complete input history is not identical. The captures stop at the decision; the auxiliary full-answer labels were not reverified there. Those records include previously disclosed auxiliary/no-fit exposure and a withdrawn reference construction. I read their summaries only, not their raw observations, and use them solely to prevent treating identical response wording as sufficient for identical internal computation or decisions.

The prospective reciprocal context intervention at exact 175 addresses a remaining local uncertainty: whether the two naturally generated pre-sign frames can change the sign while the receiving prompt and correct operands remain fixed. Its source review is recorded separately in `2026-11-17-descent-context-review.md/.json`. A reciprocal flip would establish sufficiency of these context bundles on these two prompts; a nonflip would weaken those particular interventions, not prove that context is irrelevant or isolate numbers from the rest of the question. Neither outcome proves transfer of a general descent capability. The full physical calculation after the free sign remains a separate readback; no sign score becomes the fitting objective, stopping threshold or correspondence criterion.

The four original papers and Final Experiment keep the scientific distinction intact: computational differences may guide persistent weight repair, while reliable preservation and transfer still require observation beyond the fitted path. The present evidence supports repaired fitted arithmetic and partial development transfer, with a known unrepaired descent operation. It supplies no reason to change the selected reference or to claim that matching its trace alone guarantees general arithmetic correctness.

## How the historical results guide training

The research question for this phase is how to reproduce a demonstrated repair, preserve useful computation, and carry the repair to other inputs. The November 11 diary already set that direction. The earlier version of this review described the evidence for the current learner but gave too little weight to the earlier interventions. In particular, the path-edit paper reports an overlap-derived repair on the model’s own generations. ## What we fitted

One fixed, selected most-correct original response provides the reference. Each updated model generates again from the one selected failing prompt. For each consumed position, each of 35 blocks contributes three changes in normalized residual direction. The complete field of 105 changes supplies the computational alignment. For each matched pair and merge, the excess vector distance beyond its reference tolerance determines its own loss. Contributions are weighted for repeated matches. Joint differentiation through the trace-producing operations sums their distinct contributions to each shared parameter. All 538 eligible learned arrays are in scope inside the existing architecture. Other nominally correct responses supplied the original tolerance calibration. They do not add training objectives.

The finite update uses the reconstructed derivative at the observed native computation. Fresh acquisition then uses the installed, natively rounded weights. Neither discrete generation nor the new alignment is differentiated through. Some original calibration cells have zero radius. They confer no allowance for movement at those cells. The extent to which that calibration limits learning is unresolved. Changing it would require a new loss definition and a comparable baseline. ## Where the large increases occur

The two transitions onto the 631-event history occurred at 321 and 345. The first different consumed input occupied acquisition position 176, indexed from zero, both times. The 176 shared preceding correspondence pairs retained identical mass. Their total loss changed by −2.60 × 10⁻⁹ and +2.87 × 10⁻⁹. The full-path increases were +6.79 × 10⁻⁴ and +6.75 × 10⁻⁴.

The first excursion review identified nine reference positions, 176–181, 320–321 and 542. They accounted for 88.90% of the first net rise and 89.14% of the later rise. The cosine between the two signed change maps over all 560 reference positions and 105 merges was 0.999965. A repeated input history is itself an explanation for similar maps. The two signed loss maps show a reproducible failure on this prompt. It does not yet identify which weight caused the switch or establish a recurring capability across questions.

A change in alignment alone cannot account for either spike. Two matched pairs at positions 176 and 177 retained their exact coordinates and mass, yet their local loss accounted for 23.67% and 23.79% of the two net increases. Their consumed inputs can differ across histories. For the remaining change, the saved records mix new computational content with new alignment. Partition of both rises.

There is a second limitation even on the short route. The combined contribution of the two pairs matching reference positions 373 and 374 to current position 374 at the first merge stayed near 0.0000204588 from 317 to 344, about one third of total loss. Its relative change was below two parts in ten million. A lower aggregate can coexist with a sizable persistent local error.

The weight records give a concrete reason to investigate this stubborn error. Across all 19 transitions with that exact two-cell attribution through 349, 92.21% of its squared parameter-gradient norm lay in the token embedding table. The native table accumulated just 59 changed entries counted across those transitions. At 349 it changed zero stored entries while its FP32 remainder accumulated. The two cells improved by only 1.17 × 10⁻¹³ while aggregate loss fell 4.75 × 10⁻⁸. This local discrepancy has a gradient, and the finite native update barely moved it. The data do not establish that quantization alone accounts for the residual. Early-merge weight attribution.

## What the evidence warrants

Continuing the one-pair training is justified by measured fresh decreases. The comparison so far supports a local training effect, with limited net improvement in this full-weight phase and substantial dependence on the generated history. It supplies no estimate of the fraction of distance to capability repair. Persisting loss, reversals and unchanged local components remain visible in the complete series.

A discriminating causal comparison would replay the two observed histories through the same frozen native weights using identical acquisition geometry. Measure both against the same fixed reference, recomputing computational correspondence, and measure the ordinary free generation separately. Swapping histories at fixed weights would isolate their conditional contribution. Repeating the comparison at a second fixed state would expose the weight contribution and its interaction with the history. Replay scores would be diagnostic only. This comparison has not been run for these two episodes.

An earlier full-path procedure repaired 9 of 31 previously wrong responses and damaged 6 of 9 previously correct responses on a separate set that researchers subsequently saw. That historical result is a reason to measure both repair and damage. It came from a different procedure and cannot establish transfer for this run. [Historical repair and transfer records](../2026-11-09-round-two-repairs/STUDY.md).

Capability repair further requires a frozen edited checkpoint and preselected independent failures and successes. Compare the original and edited weights on the same cases. Improvement on previously unused failures with preserved successful computations would support transfer. Damage to successes or a benefit confined to the fitted prompt would narrow the claim even if fitted loss fell. Keep those cases outside training and checkpoint selection.

# Decode mechanism world

## Direction
A strategy-game cutaway world for recorded GPU execution. The scene, not a page, is the interface: connected layer mechanisms occupy a continuous industrial floor. The user explicitly replaces the earlier daylight report, its card grid and prose inspector. Mode: Experience, with exact operating controls.

## First viewport
A full-screen oblique scene fills the 3840×2050 page area. Several neighboring mechanisms are visible; layer titles and measured timings belong to their machines. No hero, stacked overview, or permanent explanation column. A thin top bar holds recording and navigation. A compact transport and layer strip occupy the bottom; the corner minimap shows the complete recorded stack and camera location.

## Signature interaction
Pan across connected layers, wheel to zoom, Shift-drag to orbit, double-click a housing to enter it. A World control pulls back to the entire sequence. Follow playback moves the camera only while enabled; a manual camera move disables following. Select another layer and compare it beside the first in two equally sized scenes with a shared recorded clock. Escape returns one level; keyboard buttons expose every action.

## Material and encoding
Dark graphite floor, machined slate housings, pale labels, teal recorded work and amber waits. Selection is a white outline, not a new timing category. Storage has stacked ribs; tensor-core products have tile matrices; reductions use converging lanes; nonlinear stages use curves. These drawings describe operations, not simulated tensor values, bandwidth or chip placement. Activity fills only while recorded intervals contain the cursor. Moving marks indicate cursor position within an observed span, never imagined operand travel.

## Evidence
Own measured duration, shared-span operation and untimed storage are distinct. Every recorded step remains reachable through an optional step list and per-block evidence drawer. Root-step timing controls token playback; children are never added to parent cost. NCU/nsys/source evidence remains a separate drawer and its own clock.

## Lifecycle extension
The same world includes an intake station, output station and logical return route. Each layer has separate supply, compute and publication tracks; wait gates have twin rails and shared-cache portals have dashed insets. Intake/output remain grey and untimed. PLE and publication carry their enclosing-span label, never a second cost. The selected layer reveals its supply/shared routes; World shows the stack and return without a cloud of every side connection. Independent snapshots open on demand, not as a fabricated continuous history.

## Layout and controls
One viewport, no document scrolling. On phones, the same world pans beneath the viewport; comparison uses two vertical scene panes. The minimap collapses, selectors condense and the evidence drawer uses available height. Space plays/pauses; arrows pan; +/− zoom; Escape backs out. Reduced motion starts paused and camera framing is immediate. No ambient animation, remote assets or dependencies.

## Direction reasoning
The user's navigable game-world requirement is pinned. The continuous cutaway topology from the cross-section-world challenger carries it best: neighboring mechanisms remain visible while one opens. The loom contributes traceability from aggregate to instruction; the reference-sheet challenger contributes synchronized side-by-side views; the arcade challenger contributes strict separation of HUD and playfield. Their literal textile, paper and pixel costumes are not used. The original prose-heavy dashboard and the opposite neon KPI dashboard are both rejected.

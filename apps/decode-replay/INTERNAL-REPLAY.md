# Internal decode replay: mechanism-world goal

The operator must be able to choose a recorded token, walk each layer's internal work and waits in a connected graph, open each step to see what it does and its measured duration, and identify long work, publication skew and waiting. A static inventory or a blinking whole-kernel launch does not meet this goal.

The data path is the existing raw phase-tape reader, with its launch binding, geometry, ownership and timestamp-wrap checks intact. Its per-layer owner model must support E2B's changing FFN width. The reader exports measured internal intervals and per-block observations rather than only aggregate distributions. Source metadata explains operations; it never supplies invented timings.

The primary surface is now one full-viewport, navigable mechanism world. The operator rejected the stacked report because it was hard to compare side by side and carried too much prose: "Think of a videogame." Adjacent layer mechanisms remain in one spatial scene; pan, zoom, orbit and enter parts without scrolling a document. A two-pane comparison keeps both mechanisms visible at once. Compact timing sits on machinery; descriptions and raw block evidence open only on request. A token transport and minimap stay over the scene. The operator clarified that 3D means an exploded working mechanism, like looking inside a car engine, not a 3D timeline. The cutaway therefore exposes computational parts, tensor inputs/outputs, weight/cache/state storage, residual bypasses and nested operations. Parts open into measured substeps, then into explained operations within shared timing spans. Launch-level Nsight data and NCU counters are supporting evidence, not substitutes for internal execution.

The whole-token level sits above the layer cutaway: every recorded layer stays visible with its measured internal steps, one transport spans the entire token, and playback follows layer completion boundaries into the active layer's substeps without restarting at each layer. Embedding, input work between stamps, final logits and sampling remain visibly untimed where the tape does not cover them.

The lifecycle extension exposes token intake, embedding, E2B per-layer-input preparation, final normalization/readout/selection, stop/advance and the logical feedback loop. Layer input normalization/staging and output publication/readiness become machinery. E2B PLE injection and KV-sharing connections come from the hash-bound capture tables, never a guessed model label or layer count. Transport and snapshots have visible evidence limits: no invented transfer duration, readiness attribution, cache contents, or continuity between separate recordings. The existing layer clock and all recorded spans remain intact.

Evidence must distinguish a directly measured interval, a measured span containing several unseparated operations, and an operation without timestamps. Gaps remain explicit, but the available internal captures must be decoded and shown rather than relegated to a warning.

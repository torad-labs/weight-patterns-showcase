Training on Local Divergence Between Computational Traces

Marcos Damasceno Torad Labs

Abstract

Earlier weight edits repaired reasoning on problems outside their fit and sometimes damaged computations that had worked. We study how to make the repair procedure reproducible. We measure the change in direction through each residual merge during generation, align a failing execution to a fixed successful execution, and differentiate each local discrepancy beyond the variation seen in correct traces. The same rule supplies the gradient to shared weights throughout the model. In Gemma 4 E2B, it changed a downhill energy calculation while preserving the subsequent subtraction of friction. Across eight new formulations of four physical configurations, a frozen endpoint produced three correct numerical chains, versus one at baseline. All three came from the same prompt template. Two earlier checkpoints each produced two correct chains even though the second had 56% lower fitted loss. On a separately recorded weight update, total trace loss fell by 0.29% while the pre-decision computation moved farther into the observed error direction. The unrounded gradient already predicted this local increase. In a second prospective panel, eleven updates incorporating a direction shared by two successful edits preserved the fitted calculation and produced no new correct descent on eight fresh prompts.

The physics behind the sign

Let 𝐷=𝑚𝑔𝑑 be the positive energy released on a drop of depth 𝑑, 𝑄 the energy dissipated by friction, and 𝐻 the height of the subsequent climb. In this setup the block has enough energy to climb if

𝐾available=𝐾0+𝐷−𝑄 ≥ 𝑚𝑔𝐻.

On the 96 J problem, both 55 J and the correct 115 J exceed the requirement. A grader that sees only “yes” rewards either calculation. We therefore audit the sign with its operand convention, the intermediate quantities and the verdict. We also measure the trace loss independently. The arithmetic checks whether a response obeys conservation of energy. Only the internal trace discrepancy supplies the training gradient.

Our working hypothesis is that correct uses of a capability share measurable internal operations across different solutions, and that editing the weights responsible for a recurring departure can repair the capability. A fitted repair tests the intervention on one trajectory. Transfer asks whether a second trajectory reaches a corresponding operation and benefits from the same installed weights.

1. What reaches the weights

Earlier stages of this program isolated recurring trace signals for two errors in these energy problems (adding an earlier drop to the climb height and subtracting gravitational work on descent) and showed unseen transfer: FK and A2 repaired 15 of 20 climb fields and 9 of 31 full chains on 40 excluded prompts, and 60-trace DTW overlap a2-path reversed 29 of 30 fitted downhill signs and 7 of 21 excluded failures (§13). Because those one-shot solves traded collateral regressions, we test iterative computational backpropagation here. [P]

We used one fixed reference and trained on one freely generated failing response from the same failing prompt at every update. The reference was the originally successful trace with the smallest mean squared computational difference to the other successes after alignment. It computes 16+40−12=44 J and a 20 J climb correctly, although it calls the energy released by the descent “potential energy gained.” The fitted problem requires 64+40−13=91 J. The original model reports 11 J. Fourteen other originally successful traces calibrate acceptable variation. Their answers never enter as further targets for the weight derivative. [M]

Measuring the effect of one branch

At each consumed position 𝑗, the model adds three branches to the residual stream in each of its 35 transformer blocks. These additions carry the attention output, the feed-forward output and a per-layer input branch. For each of these 105 additions, indexed by 𝑚, we measure the change in direction between its incoming and outgoing 1,536-dimensional vectors:

𝑇𝑗𝑚=ℎout‖ℎout‖−ℎin‖ℎin‖.

This “turn” retains the size of the change in direction. It discards the absolute sizes of the incoming and outgoing states. Two distinct internal calculations can produce the same turn. We flatten all 105 turns at each position and align the two sequences by dynamic time warping with cost one minus cosine similarity. The path 𝑃 spans both complete histories, including the prompt. Its entries pair positions by computation. The matcher receives no words or token-equality labels.

Assigning the discrepancy to the weights

Write 𝑅𝑖𝑚 for the frozen reference turn, 𝛿𝑖𝑗𝑚=𝑇𝑗𝑚−𝑅𝑖𝑚, and 𝑑𝑖𝑗𝑚=‖𝛿𝑖𝑗𝑚‖. The allowance 𝜏𝑖𝑚 is the square root of twice the mean squared distance of the fourteen calibration traces, first averaged within each trace’s matches. A larger deviation contributes its own squared excess. With 𝑁=560 reference positions and 𝑛𝑖 matches for position 𝑖,

𝑤𝑖=1105𝑁𝑛𝑖,𝐿=∑(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝑃∑𝑚𝑤𝑖[𝑑𝑖𝑗𝑚−𝜏𝑖𝑚]+2.

Here [𝑥]+=max(𝑥,0). Outside the allowance the local trace derivative is 2𝑤𝑖(1−𝜏𝑖𝑚𝑑𝑖𝑗𝑚)𝛿𝑖𝑗𝑚. Inside the allowance it is zero. A small excess therefore contributes a smaller trace derivative than a large excess at the same weight. On the current history and alignment, the chain rule sends all these contributions into shared weights through 𝑔=𝐽𝑇⊤∇𝑇𝐿. The Jacobian includes both sides of each measured residual addition. The expanded learner makes 538 parameter tensors eligible, including the accessed embedding rows. Earlier phases restricted the eligible weights, as described on the next page.

2. What changed during training

The update uses the measured response along 𝑠=−𝑔. Stack the weighted vector excesses into 𝑟, so that 𝐿=‖𝑟‖2, and let 𝑞=𝐽𝑟𝑠. The stationary step along this direction in the local linear approximation has length 𝛼=−𝑟⊤𝑞𝑞⊤𝑞. The proposed displacement is 𝜂𝛼𝑠, with the empirically calibrated multiplier 𝜂=0.26989. The installer rounds to each tensor’s native precision and carries the difference into the next update. The model then generates freely from the edited weights and the full computation is paired afresh. Differentiation follows the current history through a surrogate for finite-precision rounding. A new greedy choice can take the model onto a different history. [M, H]

9. Can overlap predict the scope of a repair?

A correct friction calculation can appear in a globally wrong response. Once this is visible, a prediction becomes possible. An edit aimed at the recurrent descent departure might repair the descent and preserve friction. On another trajectory, an erroneous operation shared by both groups might persist because their difference provides no training signal there. These are predictions about named operations. The 387→388 comparison suggested this distinction after the outcomes were available.

For a differentiable shared trace feature 𝑆 on a held history, an installed displacement Δ𝜃 gives a local approximation

Δ𝑆≈𝐽𝑆Δ𝜃.

Even if the local loss at 𝑆 is zero, a weight shared with a divergent operation can move 𝑆. Regeneration can also change which history reaches the operation. Similar correct and wrong traces identify a candidate for preservation or persistence. Their overlap alone sets no bound on the size of the change. The earlier many-trace experiment made precisely such a preservation forecast and measured substantial sideways movement outside the targeted region (§13).

A prediction that can fail

Before installing an edit, freeze the reference bank, stage matcher, candidate update and unseen problem families. For each operation in the original responses, record its starting validity, the measured trace evidence and a prediction of repair, persistence, preservation or regression. Record unresolved cases too. A correct friction step inherited from an erroneous descent starts in the preservation group. A second incorrect operation that both reference and query perform incorrectly starts in the persistence or repair group, depending on the evidence. An unexplained failure of the targeted descent remains a failure on that task.

Regenerate each held prompt freely from the installed weights and audit each operation from its own inputs. Report how often the forecasts were correct separately for each outcome, how often the method abstained, which operations disappeared or became unreadable, and how often a rule that predicts no change would have been right on the same observations. Include initially failing friction cases and examples with different orderings. In a sample where every friction calculation already works, universal preservation is an easy prediction. Hold whole physical configurations together and reserve model and domain changes for independent tests. The companion protocol specifies this comparison. No prospective operation-by-operation forecast accuracy is reported here.

At baseline only 33 of 7,402 registered contributions fell below the floor. Six rounded steps were predicted to increase the auxiliary loss, and five did. Four continuous directions were already uphill at infinitesimal length. [N]

Independent physics on new configurations

Four unseen configurations each appeared in two orders. Each fell one joule above or below the energy needed to climb. Two readers independently scored all sixteen free generations with checkpoint assignments concealed. Neither found a newly correct descent or a complete local chain. Six of eight generations at each checkpoint reached the decoding cap.

One answer derived the correct 45 J bottom kinetic energy while stating that gravity did negative work. Another chose the initial height as its energy datum. Its 41 J of total energy against 40 J of final potential gives the same margin as 61 J available against 60 J required. At 390 it reached the correct verdict. At 401 the same prompt ended at the cap before the verdict. All sixteen answers numerically subtracted the correct friction amount from their own operand. The two blind audits document differences in signed formulas, sequence and reuse. [N]

All 105 merges were captured. On source locations visited by both checkpoints, prefix loss rose in six of eight pairs. The 59 J problem gave both extremes across its two orderings. Neither acquired the missing downhill operation. The three unchanged answer sequences also had higher matched loss. [N]

13. Scope and evidence

How this differs from the earlier edits

Earlier stages established stored-weight repair and unseen transfer on both energy failures using one-shot matrix solves. On the climb error, the single-pair rank-one edit D1 (one attention-output matrix) preserved two repairs, a protected case and seven prior correct chains while cutting root-mean-square action on four unrelated background requests by 85.5% versus the initial edit, with no gain on four previously examined physics prompts. Using contextual trace targets, three-matrix FK repaired 15 of 20 wrong or missing climb fields across 40 excluded prompts (2 of 31 full chains; 8 of 9 prior successes damaged), and 46-matrix A2 repaired 25 of 36 fitted failures and 9 of 31 unseen chains (15 of 20 climb fields) on that previously examined 40-question set while damaging 6 of 9 prior successes. Separately, a 105-merge conditional climb overlap from 13 wrong-descent donors separated 643 of 673 cells on the withheld 115 J climb error and dropped 86.9% across 670 shared cells after checkpoint 175 repaired that climb from 60 to 30 J. On the descent sign, 18-matrix a2-path (solved from a 60-trace computational DTW overlap without token labels) repaired 29 of 30 fitted signs (17 complete) and 7 of 21 excluded Round-1 failures (11 of 21 adding descent), while 12 of 30 overlap successes and 4 of 7 Round-1 successes regressed. Outside its targeted interval, regenerated traces drifted sideways before aligned position 142 by 2.6–2.7 local gaps; inside it, wrong traces moved 77% along the intended turn direction while full distance from the original correct pattern grew 16%. Those one-shot solves proved transferable trace signals while exposing collateral drift across coupled layers, motivating iterative computational backpropagation and operation-level localization. Their objectives, data and weights differ from this learner and cannot be pooled into one success rate. [P]

When to stop following a single reference

The final 42 updates lowered the fitted loss by 0.23% from 348 to 390. The fitted arithmetic was already correct at 348 and failed temporarily again at 387. Even on a held 612-position history, one query position was paired to two reference positions with disjoint tolerance regions at 37 merges. Under that fixed correspondence, the resulting loss floor was 0.00000288720, or at most a 99.0124% decrease from the original fitted loss. A different alignment can change the floor. Neither this bound nor an arbitrary percentage determines when the model has acquired the operation. A stopping decision should use fresh loss and check the operation, stability under further updates and preservation on other prompts as separate outcomes. An operational stopping rule can measure the same whole-trace discrepancy on a fixed development panel, compare it with repeat-measurement variation, and save a checkpoint when gains stall or previously accepted computations move persistently outside tolerance. A domain evaluator can inspect the selected checkpoint independently. The longest observed fitted dry spell that subsequently recovered was ten updates in the 348–390 segment. That segment supplies an eleven-update check interval and a 42-update budget for this implementation. How long a first repair persists on new physical configurations remains unmeasured. [H, V]

Relation to earlier methods

Romero et al. trained a student network using intermediate representations from a teacher. Here the reference is one successful trajectory from the same original model, and the inputs and generated histories can differ. Meng et al. used causal interventions to locate and edit factual associations in GPT. Our weight-family interventions ask a related sufficiency question about a multi-step calculation. Neither comparison supplies a benchmark for computational cost. We have measured one model, a narrow physics domain and deterministic generations. An advantage over reinforcement learning, or a general change in the geometry of the model’s loss landscape, remains unmeasured.

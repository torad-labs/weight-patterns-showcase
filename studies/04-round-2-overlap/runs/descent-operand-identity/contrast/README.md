# A change in later friction can reverse the earlier downhill calculation

Among 30 public original responses, there were 18 matched pairs that differed only in the friction-loss number, spanning 22 distinct questions and nine fixed physical configurations. In nine pairs the model also reversed how it applied the *unchanged* downhill work. Increasing friction changed an incorrect subtraction to a net addition five times and a net addition to an incorrect subtraction four times. These are overlapping pairs of deterministic model responses under different prompts. They are not 18 independent trials. [Results and exact question IDs](result.json)

One example starts with 64 J of kinetic energy and gains 40 J going downhill. When the rough section cost 13 J, the model computed 24 J at the bottom. When the rough section cost 24 J, it computed 104 J at the bottom. All other prompt text and numerical parameters stayed the same. The physically correct bottom energy is 104 J at both friction values. The model's friction application itself subtracts the rough-section loss correctly in each run.

In the nine friction contrasts where the model used the same local arithmetic convention, bottom kinetic energy was unchanged and the later available energy fell by exactly one joule for every additional joule of dissipation. Across the 105-merge trace measured immediately before each application, the friction event changed more than the descent event in only four of those nine pairs. Median per-pair friction-minus-descent Frobenius sensitivity was −0.04727 per joule. In the nine pairs that switched arithmetic convention, friction changed more in seven. Across the 30 comparisons of same-convention treatment pairs sharing no question, the median cosine between the signed friction perturbation fields was 0.0011 at friction and 0.0011 at descent. Individual comparisons reuse pairs. The whole-field norm and signed direction at these two event snapshots do not supply a consistent operand-specific response in these controls. [Pairwise and per-layer changes](result.json)

Only two pairs differed in drop height alone. One retained an incorrect subtraction at both heights. The other switched from valid addition to wrong subtraction. Neither pair retained a valid downhill calculation at both endpoints. The observed bottom energy changes were respectively −1 J/J and −3 J/J of additional downhill work. A physical response of +1 J/J on a stable successful route cannot be estimated from these pairs.

Each file-backed comparison checked the complete prompt after replacing its changed field, recorded the changed native input token span, verified the model, decoding configuration, saved result, and SHA-256 of the turn field. The independently marked operation events supplied evaluation timing; each measurement used the full 105-merge transformation field at the preceding native prediction. The result contains 35 per-layer and 105 per-merge sensitivities for each operand and every pair. Different generated histories, changes in both operands' running states, and perturbations to a complete prompt can all change a trace. The observed sign reversal is an effect of the prompt variation in these matched responses. Its particular weight-level cause and the earliest responsible computation remain open. Prospective method · Measurement

A follow-up oriented the nine sign-switching contrasts from an incorrect to a correct descent. Across the 15 pairwise comparisons among their six distinct fixed configurations, the median whole-field alignment was 0.129 at descent and 0.175 at friction. In the eight comparisons where increasing the dissipation had opposite effects on downhill correctness, just five descent alignments were positive, with median cosine 0.106. This decomposition does not reveal a common direction specific to gravitational work. It does provide matched inputs where an irrelevant-to-descent amount flips the computation without changing the physical downhill operand.
